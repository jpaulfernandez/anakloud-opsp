# Anakloud — Founder Alignment Baseline

**Purpose of this document:** the question set each team member answers privately, what each question is for, and how the answers roll up into a One-Page Strategic Plan you study together afterwards.

**Status:** questions only. Nothing built yet.

**Two rules for how this gets used**

1. Everyone answers alone, before any group discussion. The whole value is in catching disagreement you don't know you have. One conversation before the form and you've contaminated the sample.
2. The output is a **baseline, not a decision**. Put that sentence at the top of the form itself, not just in the group chat. People answer honestly when they know the artifact can't be quoted back at them in six months.

**A note on the fourth app.** You couldn't remember its name. That's not a knock — it's data. If a fourth product isn't top-of-mind for someone actively facilitating strategy, it's worth finding out whether it's top-of-mind for anyone. Q5 will tell you. Fill in its real name before sending; a blank there biases the ranking.

**A note on your MMDC rocks.** "Make MMDC understood," "make it easy," "balance rigor and fun" — these work well as *themes*. They're memorable and they steer judgment. But you can't tell in December whether "make it easy" got done. For Anakloud, use both: a theme phrase (Q9) that people can repeat, and rocks (Q8) with a number attached. Theme for direction, rocks for done.

## Part A — The question set

13 questions, roughly 25 minutes. Confidence ratings on the ones marked ⓘ. Q11–Q13 are cuttable if you need it shorter — see Part D.

### Section 1 — Why this exists

#### Q1. Why does Anakloud need to exist?

> In 2–3 sentences: what is broken in the world right now that Anakloud fixes? Write it the way you'd explain it to a friend who isn't in tech.

**Type:** Long text (min 200 characters, to prevent one-liners)
**Interaction:** Plain textarea. No examples, no placeholder text — placeholder text is the most common accidental anchor.
**Maps to:** OPSP → Purpose

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"A parent in Cavite waits four to six months for a developmental assessment, then travels two hours each way for weekly sessions. Most give up. We want the waiting and the travelling to stop being the reason a child doesn't get therapy."*
- *"Therapy centers run on notebooks, Google Sheets and Viber groups. Nobody can tell you how many active clients they have. We give them the system they'd have if someone had built software for this market."*
- *"Parents pay for months of therapy and never actually know if it's working. They just trust. We make progress visible so they can see what they're buying."*

**How to read it:** These are three different companies. The first is a healthcare access mission — it implies scale, subsidy, eventually government or HMO. The second is a B2B SaaS play — it implies sales, pricing, churn. The third is a consumer trust play — it implies the parent is the customer. If your team splits across these, every downstream disagreement about roadmap and pricing traces back here. Fix this first or you'll re-argue it forever in disguise.

#### Q2. If Anakloud disappeared tonight, who notices first?

> Finish the sentence: "The people who would miss it most are ______, because ______."

**Type:** Two short text fields
**Interaction:** Sentence-completion, two inline blanks
**Maps to:** OPSP → Purpose (cross-check on Q1)

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Therapy center admins, because they'd go back to reconstructing schedules by hand every morning."*
- *"Honestly, nobody yet. We haven't earned that."*
- *"Pediatricians, because they'd lose the only way to know whether the child they referred ever showed up."*

**How to read it:** This is a lie detector on Q1. Someone can write a beautiful mission in Q1 and then name a completely different beneficiary here. Also, anyone brave enough to answer "nobody yet" is being useful — don't let the group treat it as negativity.

### Section 2 — Where we're going

#### Q3. The number that would prove it worked ⓘ

> Three years from now, if Anakloud is working, what is the **one number** that proves it? Name the metric, then give the value. Then one line: why that number and not another?

**Type:** Short text (metric name) + number + unit + short text (why)
**Interaction:** Three fields side by side, then a one-line reason. **Deliberately no examples given.** Confidence slider 1–5.
**Maps to:** OPSP → 3-Year Targets

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Children with an active therapy plan — 10,000. Because that's the only number that means we changed something for a kid."*
- *"Paying therapy centers — 300. Everything else follows from this; centers are the distribution."*
- *"Monthly recurring revenue — ₱2M. If we can't get here we're a school project with extra steps."*
- *"Pediatricians referring monthly — 500. The referral is the scarce thing, not the software."*

**How to read it:** **Don't look at the numbers, look at the units.** Four people picking four different units means the team has never agreed on what winning is. That is completely normal at your stage and completely fatal if left alone. The unit someone picks reveals which company they think they joined — impact, distribution, revenue, or supply.

If everyone picks the same unit and different numbers, you're in good shape; that's just a forecasting conversation.

#### Q4. Ten years, not three ⓘ

> Same question, but ten years out and allowed to be unreasonable. One sentence. If it doesn't feel slightly embarrassing to write down, it's too small.

**Type:** Short text (one sentence, 140 char cap)
**Interaction:** Single line with character counter. Confidence slider 1–5.
**Maps to:** OPSP → BHAG (draft only)

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Every child in the Philippines with a developmental delay is identified before age five."*
- *"The default operating system for pediatric therapy in Southeast Asia."*
- *"I can't answer this honestly yet and I'd rather say so."*

**How to read it:** Expect low confidence, and that's fine — you're pre-beta, a real BHAG isn't earned yet. The reason to ask anyway is that the *flavour* of the answers tells you something even when the substance is guesswork. Someone reaching for "every child in the Philippines" and someone reaching for "SEA market leader" are motivated by different things, and knowing that helps you assign roles. **Do not write the BHAG into the OPSP in ink this round.** Note it in pencil and revisit after beta.

### Section 3 — Who we're for

#### Q5. The four roles

> For each group below, mark which of the four things they do. A group can do more than one, or none.

**Type:** Matrix / grid, checkboxes
**Interaction:** Rows × columns grid. Rows: pediatrician / developmental pedia, therapy center owner or director, occupational therapist, speech-language pathologist, parent or guardian, school or SPED teacher, the child, LGU or DOH program, HMO or insurer. Columns: **Pays us · Decides to adopt · Uses it most days · Benefits most**.
**Maps to:** OPSP → Sandbox (Core Customer)

**What plausible answers look like:**

| | Pays | Decides | Uses daily | Benefits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Respondent A* | Center owner | Center owner | Therapist | Child |
| *Respondent B* | Center owner | **Pediatrician** | Therapist | Parent |
| *Respondent C* | **Parent** | Parent | Parent | Child |

**How to read it:** "Pays" and "uses daily" will probably align across the team. **"Decides" is where it breaks.** If half the team thinks the center owner decides and half thinks the pediatrician decides, you have two incompatible go-to-market motions and you're about to build for both. That's the most expensive mistake available to you right now, and this grid catches it for free.

#### Q6. The tiebreak

> The therapy center wants one thing. The parent wants the opposite. We can only serve one. Whose side do we take? One line: why.

**Type:** Single choice (center / parent / pediatrician / therapist) + required short text
**Interaction:** Radio buttons, "why" field required — do not let them submit a bare choice
**Maps to:** OPSP → Core Customer

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Center. They pay, and if they churn there's no data for the parent to look at anyway."*
- *"Parent. Demand comes from parents. Centers will adopt whatever parents are already asking for."*
- *"Pediatrician. They're the referral source; both the center and the parent are downstream of them."*

**How to read it:** This is the sharpest question in the set and the one most likely to produce a genuinely uncomfortable silence in the alignment session. That discomfort is the point. "We serve everyone" is not an available answer — an ecosystem of four apps is exactly the kind of product that dies of trying.

---

#### Q7. Why us and not the notebook ⓘ

> Finish this sentence in one line, max 120 characters: "A therapy center should switch from their notebook, Excel and Viber group to Anakloud because we are the only ones who ______."

**Type:** Short text, hard 120-character limit
**Interaction:** Single line, live character counter. Confidence slider 1–5.
**Maps to:** OPSP → Brand Promise

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"...show the parent, the therapist and the referring doctor the same progress record, live."*
- *"...cut two hours of admin per therapist per week."*
- *"...connect the referral, the sessions and the progress report in one thread."*
- *"...are built specifically for Filipino pediatric therapy, not adapted from a US EMR."*

**How to read it:** The character limit is doing real work — it forces a choice between "we do many things" and "we do this one thing." Watch for answers that are really features rather than promises. "We have scheduling and billing" is a feature list. "You'll never lose a referral again" is a promise. Also note: the strongest competitor here isn't another app, it's the notebook. Say that out loud in the session.

### Section 4 — Focus

#### Q8. Which door opens first ⓘ

> Rank the four apps by which one gets a customer to say yes *first*. Not which is most important long-term — which one opens the door.
>
> Then: if we had to delete one entirely and ship three, which goes? One line why.

**Type:** Ranking (drag or numbered 1–4) + single choice + short text
**Interaction:** Rank PedConnect, TeachDay, ParentUp, [fourth app]. Then a separate "delete one" radio. Confidence slider 1–5. **Also ask: "what do you think the group's #1 will be?"** — a second ranking field.
**Maps to:** OPSP → Key Initiatives, Quarterly Priorities

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"PedConnect first. The referral is the scarce resource. A center with no incoming referrals doesn't need software to manage them."*
- *"TeachDay first. Centers have the money and the daily pain. Doctors won't change a fifteen-year referral habit for a startup with no track record."*
- *"ParentUp first. Parents are the only ones who'll adopt something new without being sold to. They'll drag the centers in."*
- *"Delete: [fourth app]. I had to check the deck to remember what it does."*

**How to read it:** This may be the single highest-value question on the form for where you are right now. Whichever app leads determines your entire beta design, your first sales conversation, and what "success" means in ninety days. If the team splits here, everything downstream sits on sand.

The "predict the group" field is a quiet gem: if four people privately rank TeachDay first but each predicts the group will say PedConnect, you've found a silent majority that's been deferring to someone.

#### Q9. What we are deliberately not doing

> Name three things Anakloud will **not** do in the next two years, even though they're tempting and someone will suggest them.

**Type:** Three short text fields
**Interaction:** Three separate lines, all required
**Maps to:** OPSP → Sandbox boundaries

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Not delivering teletherapy ourselves. We're the rails, not the therapist."*
- *"Not adult rehab, even though the software would mostly work."*
- *"No expansion outside the Philippines before 200 centers."*
- *"Not building our own billing/payments. Integrate."*
- *"Not selling to hospitals. Different sales cycle, different product."*

**How to read it:** Strategy is mostly a list of refusals, and this is the only question that gets at them directly. Also watch for someone naming something the team has *already started building* as a thing you shouldn't do. That's a conversation waiting to happen, and better now than after another sprint.

### Section 5 — Money

#### Q10. How the money works ⓘ

> Four parts.
>
> a) Who physically pays us?
> b) What's the model?
> c) What do they pay, in pesos?
> d) What month does the first real peso arrive — not a grant, not a competition prize, a customer paying for the product?

**Type:** Single choice + single choice + number (₱) + month picker
**Interaction:**
- (a) radio: center · parent · pediatrician/clinic · school · LGU/DOH · HMO · other
- (b) radio: monthly subscription per center · per-seat/per-therapist · per active child per month · per session fee · freemium with parent upgrade · commission on referrals · grant or institutional funding · not sure yet
- (c) peso amount, with a unit dropdown matching (b)
- (d) month + year picker
- Confidence slider 1–5

**Maps to:** OPSP → Profit per X, 1-Year Plan → Critical Number

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Center pays. Monthly subscription. ₱2,500/month flat. First peso: January 2027."*
- *"Center pays. ₱200 per active child per month. First peso: November 2026."*
- *"Parent pays. ₱349/month, centers use it free. First peso: March 2027."*
- *"Not sure yet. First peso: honestly no idea."*

**How to read it:** Two things to look at. **The model split** — flat subscription versus per-child pricing versus consumer subscription are three different businesses with different product requirements, and teams routinely discover at month eighteen that they never agreed. **The date spread** — if answers range from November 2026 to mid-2027, some of you think this is a company and some still think it's a project. That gap needs naming out loud, kindly, because it usually maps onto how much runway people think they have.

"Not sure yet" is an acceptable answer here and shouldn't be penalised. An honest "not sure" from four people is a clearer signal than four confident guesses.

### Section 6 — The next 90 days

#### Q11. What must be done by year-end ⓘ

> Beta starts soon. Name up to three things that must be **finished** by 31 December 2026 for us to say the quarter worked.
>
> For each one, write how we'll know it's done — a number, a date, or something you could point at. "Improve onboarding" is not done-able. "Eight centers have each logged 20+ sessions" is.
>
> Then star the single most important one.

**Type:** Three paired text fields (item + done-condition) + radio to star the #1
**Interaction:** Repeating row: "What" / "Done when". Star selector below. Confidence slider 1–5.
**Maps to:** OPSP → Quarterly Priorities (Rocks) + the #1 Critical Number

**What plausible answers look like:**

| What | Done when |
|---|---|
| Onboard beta centers | 8 centers have each logged 20+ real sessions, not demo data |
| Prove the referral loop | 15 referrals complete doctor → center → parent progress view, end to end |
| Data privacy baseline | NPC registration filed, consent flow shipped, DPO named |
| Decide the wedge | We have written evidence of which app gets a yes fastest |
| Hire a clinical adviser | An OT or SLP is on retainer and reviewing our progress framework |

**How to read it:** Everyone will want to star something different, and the starred item is what actually matters — a team that agrees on a list of five and disagrees on the one has not agreed on anything. Expect the split to fall along Q8 lines: whoever ranked PedConnect first will star a doctor-side rock.

Also: if nobody names anything about privacy, compliance, or clinical validity, that's your gap. You're about to put real children's health data on a server.

### Section 7 — Risk, people, values

#### Q13. Pre-mortem

> It's early 2028. Anakloud is dead. Write the two sentences explaining what killed it.
>
> Then pick the most likely cause from the list.

**Type:** Long text + single choice
**Interaction:** Textarea, then radio: centers wouldn't change their workflow · doctors wouldn't refer through us · ran out of money · the team drifted apart · data privacy or regulatory problem · a competitor got there first · product too complex to onboard · we never picked one thing · other
**Maps to:** OPSP → SWT (Threats)

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"Centers loved every demo and never changed a single thing about how they worked. We mistook enthusiasm for adoption."*
- *"Two of us took full-time jobs in November. It went quiet by February. Nobody ever said it was over."*
- *"We spread across four apps and none of them got good enough for anyone to pay for."*
- *"A parent complaint about a child's records went to the NPC and we had nothing documented."*

**How to read it:** The hypothetical framing is what makes this safe. People will name the real fear here — including fears about each other's commitment — because it's phrased as fiction. Read these before the 1:1s. They're the best possible preparation for those conversations.

#### Q14. What you want to own, and what you think others own

> a) From the list, pick up to three functions **you** want to own. Owning means: if it goes wrong, you're the one who noticed first.
> b) For each teammate, name the one function you think **they** own.
> c) Realistically, how many hours a week can you give Anakloud from October 2026?
> d) Anything that would make you step back, that you haven't said out loud yet? *(This answer is seen only by the facilitator.)*

**Type:** Multi-select (max 3) + per-person short text + slider + long text (private)
**Interaction:**
- Function list: product · backend · mobile/frontend · QA · design/UX · **data privacy & security** · **clinical & regulatory liaison** · **sales & center partnerships** · **doctor relations** · **onboarding & customer success** · support · marketing · **finance & bookkeeping** · fundraising · legal & IP · hiring
- Hours slider: 0–60
- Mark (d) clearly as facilitator-only

**Maps to:** OPSP → Accountability (who owns what) + your resource gap list

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *A wants: product, backend, QA. B wants: product, mobile, design. C wants: product, backend, data privacy. D wants: marketing, product.*
- *Nobody picks: sales, doctor relations, onboarding, finance, clinical liaison.*
- *Hours: 40, 35, 10, 8, 20 — with two people quietly assuming everyone else is full-time.*
- *(d): "If we're still unpaid by March I need to look at a job. I don't know how to bring that up."*

**How to read it:** The empty functions are your hiring list — and note that the ones in bold above are exactly the ones a team of developers never volunteers for and a healthcare product cannot survive without. That's the "missing resources" output you said you were after.

The (b) sub-question catches ownership mismatches: if three people think C owns data privacy and C thinks they own backend, something is going to get dropped, and it'll be the thing with legal consequences.

The hours spread is the most important number on this entire form. A 40-hour person and an 8-hour person can absolutely co-found a company, but not if each assumes the other is at 40.

#### Q15. A moment worth copying *(optional)*

> Think of a time someone on this team did something that made you think "that's exactly how we should operate." What did they do? Don't name the value — tell the story.

**Type:** Long text
**Interaction:** Textarea
**Maps to:** OPSP → Core Values

**What plausible answers look like:**

- *"When the demo crashed in front of the panel, J didn't explain it away. Said 'that's a real bug, we'll fix it,' and had it fixed that night."*
- *"R spent a whole Saturday sitting in a therapy center just watching, and came back and told us half our screens were wrong."*

**How to read it:** This is Scaling Up's actual method for finding core values, and it works because values that get brainstormed produce "Integrity. Excellence. Teamwork." Values extracted from stories produce things that are true. You'll do the extraction yourself afterwards — from the two stories above you'd get something like *"Say the real thing"* and *"Go sit in the center."* Both are usable. Neither would have come out of a whiteboard.

## Part C — Running the session

**Before.** Collect all responses. Build one anonymised comparison sheet per question — answers side by side, no names, in random order. Attribution stays with you for the 1:1s. At six people everyone will guess who wrote what anyway, but unattributed on the screen keeps the first twenty minutes about ideas instead of people.

Score each question:

- **Aligned** — everyone essentially agrees
- **Soft split** — people differ, confidence is low across the board. Means nobody's thought about it yet. Resolves fast.
- **Hard split** — people differ *and* confidence is high on both sides. This is a real fault line. This is your entire agenda.

**During (about 3 hours).**

1. **Silent read, 15 min.** Everyone reads the whole comparison sheet before anyone speaks. No commentary. This is the discipline that makes the rest work.
2. **Aligned items, 15 min.** Read them out, confirm, write them into the OPSP, move on. Don't relitigate agreement — it feels productive and isn't.
3. **Hard splits, ~90 min.** One at a time, hardest first (probably Q6 or Q8). For each: before anyone argues their own position, **each person states the strongest version of the view they disagree with.** Then discuss. Then decide. Steelmanning first is the difference between a decision and a standoff.
4. **Decide, don't average.** Merging six views into a sentence everyone can technically live with produces something nobody will act on. Agree beforehand who breaks ties — and say it out loud at the start, not when you're stuck.
5. **Fill the OPSP live, 30 min.** Ink where you can, pencil where you can't, blank with a revisit date where you genuinely don't know. Blank is a legitimate answer and better than a placeholder.
6. **Name the quarter, 10 min.** Q12 answers on screen, pick or write a new one together.
7. **Close by naming the #1 priority and its owner.** If you leave the room without one, the session didn't work — schedule a second one rather than pretending.

**After.** Send the filled OPSP within 24 hours, while it's still warm. Set the review date at the top. Then start the weekly rhythm — 60 minutes, same time, and the first agenda item is the #1 priority.

**One thing to say at the start:** this baseline was written before anyone had talked to each other, on purpose. Disagreement in it is a feature, not a failure of the team. People need to hear that before they see their answer sitting next to someone else's.

## Part D — If you need it shorter

Full set: ~25 minutes. To get to 15, cut in this order:

1. **Q15** (values story) — do it verbally in the session instead
2. **Q4** (ten-year BHAG) — you're deferring the BHAG anyway
3. **Q2** (who notices) — Q1 covers most of it
4. **Q9** (not doing) — can be generated live in the room

**Never cut these four.** They carry most of the signal:

- **Q5** — the four roles grid
- **Q8** — which app opens the door
- **Q11** — the 90-day rocks and the starred #1
- **Q14** — ownership, hours, and the private question

## Appendix — anchoring check

Every question above was written to avoid leading the answer. Specifically:

- **No example numbers** in Q3 or Q4. Your original draft suggested "maybe 10,000 patients" — that would have pulled every answer toward both the unit *and* the magnitude, manufacturing exactly the consensus you're trying to test for.
- **No option list on Q1**, and no placeholder text in the textarea. Placeholder text anchors as hard as an example.
- **Q5 replaces "choose one"** with a role grid, because patient/parent/doctor/teacher aren't competing answers to one question — they're different jobs, and in health products they're almost always different people.
- **Q10's "not sure yet"** is a real option, so nobody has to invent confidence they don't have.
- **Q8 and Q3 ask for confidence**, so you can tell a real fault line from an unexamined topic.
- **Q8 asks for a group prediction**, which surfaces silent majorities.
- **Q14(d) is facilitator-only**, so the honest answer has somewhere to go.

Tell people Taglish is fine. You want how they'd actually say it, not their careful English — the careful English answers are consistently blander and less true.
